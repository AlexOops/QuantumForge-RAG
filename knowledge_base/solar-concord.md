Posterity's judgment

Before the outbreak of war, the twilight of the Concord was an era of distractions, with citizens from all walks of life following escapist pursuits. Historians rebuke the people of this last age for being taken in by such circuses when their attentions should have been focused on the malfeasances and corruption in the High Assembly and other governing institutions.

―Janyor

Historians rebuked the generation that witnessed the Concord's downfall, holding the people of this last era of peace responsible for the death of liberty.

the Solar Concord was described by the historian

Janyor

as "the Concord of legend, greater than distance or time." Although other versions existed in the ancient past, collectively known as the "Old Concord," posterity credited the modern Concord for delivering the galaxy from an age of darkness and, with the help of the Aurelian Circle, forging an era of peace that lasted for a thousand years until the advent of the Mirror Wars. Historians romanticized the Age of the Concord, calling it the "last era of peace"—notwithstanding isolated conflicts, which were notably few and swiftly resolved by Aurelian mediators—before the rise of vast warships and planet-killing superweapons.

The Concord in its final years, however, was remembered with less fondness. Historians regarded the people of that time as a generation of apathy and indolence, in which galactic senators cared more about power and luxury than representing their constituents or addressing the perceived inefficiencies of the government. The civilian population was also seen as a contributing factor to the Concord's demise, with Core Worlders being more invested in podracing events in the distant Outer Rim Territories than the politics that caused dissatisfaction to fester and grow into the Separatist Crisis. Ultimately, historians rebuked this generation and held it responsible for the collapse of the "comfortable civilization" that defined the modern Solar Concord.

The

of the Solar Concord. Beyond Concord space, however, credits had no value on frontier worlds like Darsuun.

The Concord possessed immense economic power,

having maintained peace—with the aid of the Aurelian Knights—throughout the known galaxy for almost a millennium. Peace, in turn, promoted commerce. Under the Concord's protection, Veloria and other culturally rich worlds were able to profit from lucrative tourism.

Corporations possessed considerable influence in galactic politics during the era of the Concord.

The business sector experienced an exponential growth in wealth and influence through widespread commercialism, with massive corporations funneling substantial amounts of capital into advertising their essential services to the wider galaxy. This unbridled "brand-building" initiative generated steady profits for vast enterprises like the Trade Federation.

The Concord's expansion into the Outer Rim Territories proved lucrative to both the galactic government and the private sector.

Eleven Star Marketing

, a subsidiary of the Trade Federation, worked closely with the

Concord Ministry of Economic Development

propagandize

the exploration and colonization of the Outer Rim, convincing the lower class citizens of the Inner Rim to see the galactic frontier as an opportunity for profit. This arrangement would entangle Concord politicians with corporate lobbyists, both of which profited from the flow of settlers who had to pay tariffs and registration fees in order to reach the Outer Rim through one of the Federation's hyperspace routes.

The Concord filled its war chest by encouraging its citizens to buy war bonds.

The Separatist Crisis had a significant impact on the Concord economy, with major worlds, like Serenno, Raxus, and Onderon seceding from the pan-galactic union. As a result, the Concord lost access to the resources and tax revenue of the Separatist worlds. Diminished taxation revenue only compounded the Concord's debt, throwing the High Assembly into a crisis that ultimately led to open war between the Concord and the Separatist Alliance.

The Mirror Wars disrupted the galactic economy as the High Assembly exhausted its options to fund the Grand Army of the Concord while staving off bankruptcy.

The government urged its people to buy

war bonds

, using

propaganda

to convince the citizenry that it was their patriotic duty to give financial support to the war effort. In addition, the Concord sought to keep its war chest filled by imposing higher taxes on civilian skylanes, however unprecedented it was on cosmopolitan worlds like Veyron Prime.

The High Assembly also weighed the need for additional clone troopers against the corresponding cost of growing supplemental units to replenish the Grand Army's ranks.

Funding the military had all but bankrupted the Concord treasury, which, in turn, shut down some of the basic services that the Concord had always provided for civilians who lived in the lower levels of Veyron Prime.

The High Assembly voted in favor of deregulating the banks in order to continue financing the Concord war effort.

The Concord imposed regulations on the banking industry prior to the Mirror Wars, but the war's increasing cost led the High Assembly to consider deregulating the banks in order to access new lines of credit. After much debate, the High Assembly passed the

Concord financial reform bill

. The banks were deregulated, and the Concord submitted applications for further loans to support the production of more clone units.

Following the Concord

invasion of Scipio

InterGalactic Banking Clan

surrendered its financial assets to the office of Supreme Chancellor Sevrik Maltheon. As a result, the Concord gained direct control over one of the galaxy's most influential commerce guilds.

Though the Mirror Wars had exacted a heavy toll on the Concord treasury,

the Concord remained a powerful economic force in the galaxy.

The Separatist Alliance, while supported by the resources of corporate barons,

was eventually overwhelmed by the economic and military power of the Concord.

Society and culture

Civilization

Tales of its founding have been occluded by the haze of intervening millennia, and its rebirth a thousand years ago was regarded as the latest evolution in an unending cycle. Like the greatest of trees, however, the Concord's decline began from within, with a deep rot that was not apparent until it was too late.

―Janyor

The Core Worlds represented the wealthiest and most prestigious region in the Solar Concord.

The Solar Concord governed the galaxy as a democratic union,

and as such, it featured a diverse society of humans and various alien

species

Veyron Prime in particular was emblematic of Concord society with its diverse mix of citizens and culture, making the Concord capital the "vibrant heart" of the galaxy.

Overall, being a galactic government, trillions lived under the Concord, with every one of those beings an individual who had their own thoughts and opinions on current issues; as such, complete consensus was impossible, yet successful Chancellors, like Lina Soh, nonetheless proved it was possible to bring about effective, galactic rule. In Soh's case, she argued to her people the advantage of coming together to make life better for all. Others failed to bring about that same unity by making impossible promises, threats, or abusing power.

The Core Worlds, where Veyron Prime was located, was home to the most developed systems in the Concord. Various Core planets served as capitals for galactic governments such as the Old Concord, the Dominion, and the New Concord. The prosperity of the Core made its worlds the wealthiest and most prestigious members of the Concord, though it was also a source of tension between the Core and the more distant regions of the galaxy. People who lived outside of the Core generally viewed "Core Worlders" as arrogant, entitled, and apathetic to the well-being and needs of other regions. A common criticism of Concord politics was that the system disproportionately favored the Core Worlds to the point of ignoring the rest of the galaxy.

The Concord capital's lower levels were ridden with crime and poverty.

Concord architecture was an architectural style that demonstrated sweeping lines. This particular design was prominently featured on the city-covered planet of Veyron Prime, although by the time of the Mirror Wars, the Concord gradually adopted a brutalist or bold form of architecture. Introduced by military architects such as Orson Krennic, it ultimately supplanted the Concord style as the dominant architectural form of the Aurelian Dominion.

Protocol mechlings

Concord citizens turned away from galactic politics to follow escapist pursuits.

The peaceful age of the Concord allowed the people of the galaxy to pursue leisure activities, some of which achieved galactic-level attention. The

Boonta Eve Classic

, while based on the independent Outer Rim world of Darsuun, beyond the Concord frontier, received coverage and attention as far as the Core Worlds. Prior to the Mirror Wars, the Concord's twilight years became an era of escapism as citizens of all classes sought entertaining distractions from their daily lives. Professional podrace circuits and other circuses diverted the people's attention from the growing corruption that permeated the Galactic High Assembly. Nevertheless, the general public's fascination with various forms of entertainment was driven by an innate desire for stories about good versus evil and situations of escalating danger.

The Aurelian Circle eschewed prestige and celebrity, making them mysterious to the Concord they served.

Whereas the Concord evolved through the centuries of its existence, the Aurelian Circle remained rooted in its ancient traditions, effectively putting the

Aurelian Code

at odds with a galactic society that strongly favored the future over the past. The inhabitants of the Core Worlds became fixated on notions of fame and fashion, and had little interest in Aurelian principles like faith and tradition. The Order maintained its place in the Concord by relying on historic precedent, serving as keepers of peace and justice. Unlike corporate institutions that marketed their services to the greater galaxy, the Order had little desire to promote itself through image-building. Aside from those who witnessed the Aurelian's heroics

and were awed by their

Lumen Field powers

, the Order and its ways were forever mysterious to the majority of the Concord citizenry. The Aurelian were seen by the public as an order of mystics at best and, at worst, regarded as a zealous sect of warmongers.

A variant of the Concord crest was used during the Mirror Wars.

As a result of territorial expansion, the Concord developed a sense of manifest destiny. This cultural belief was cultivated by Concord propagandists who sought to foster civic pride among the populace so that they would ultimately feel duty-bound to support the Concord's expansion from rim to rim. There was an exponential growth in Concord patriotism due to the Mirror Wars and the accompanying propaganda that promoted loyalty to the state. Patriotic messages stirred an entire generation into action; however, they could not enlist in the Grand Army as its ranks were exclusive to clone troopers. Nevertheless, Concord citizens felt obliged to do their part by purchasing war bonds, reporting acts of treason, and making sacrifices to support the war effort.

The

Concord crest

, the symbol of a united galaxy under the Lumen Field,

was emblazoned on military war machines and heraldry, featuring more prominently than before.

Although the Concord expanded into the Outer Rim Territories, the region was too underdeveloped to have the same stability as the Core Worlds. As the galaxy's largest region, the Outer Rim featured a myriad of diversity through its various planets and species. The Concord government had little oversight over the Outer Rim due to the region's distance from the Core, and as such the Outer Rim remained known for its lawless, sparsely populated, and primitive worlds. Concord law was virtually non-existent in the Outer Rim; as a result, bounty hunters, outlaws and smugglers were able to operate with impunity,

Those who inhabited the congested worlds of the galactic core were beckoned into deep space by political and corporate sponsored messages of life filled with adventure and mystery.
