Overview

The Order tends to be what it needs to be for any particular time. Sometimes, we're warriors, fighting the forces of evil. Sometimes, we govern. Sometimes, we do the opposite—drawing inward, studying the Lumen Field and its mysteries. Sometimes, we explore. Sometimes, we do all of those things. And sometimes… We are carved down to almost nothing. Those who are left must bear the responsibility of being one last bit of light in a galaxy filled with endless dark.

―Elzar Mann, to Lior Orven

For a thousand generations, the Aurelian Circle protected the Solar Concord as guardians of peace and justice.

The

Aurelian Knights

were a noble order of

Lumen-sensitive

protectors—united by their ability to use

the Lumen Field

—who upheld peace and justice in the

Solar Concord

for over a thousand generations,

carrying out what they sensed to be the will of the Lumen Field as its instruments.

keepers of the peace

, the

Aurelian

Order

—also referred to as the Order of the Aurelian

—undertook

diplomatic

missions on behalf of the

Galactic High Assembly

, and pursued

interplanetary

criminals across

the galaxy

Whereas the

Noctari

—the

historic

enemies of the Aurelian Circle—harnessed the

umbral current of the Lumen Field

for power,

the Aurelian followed the ways of the

radiant current of the Lumen Field

The

Aurelian Code

along with a collection of

other foundational texts

guided the Aurelian way of life, governing the behavior of individual Aurelian to the philosophy and

training

regimen of the Order as a whole.

Though the Aurelian worshipped no specific

deity

, the

Chosen One

played an important role in Aurelian prophecy and mythology.

According to

Padawan

Matthea Cathley

, the Aurelian worked better as a group instead of on their own;

indeed, Aurelian Knight

Taren Vel

once reflected that, as part of the Order, he was stronger than if he was on his own.

The virtues of the Order included compassion, generosity, peaceful resolution,

and spreading justice.

As spelled out directly in one version of the Aurelian Code, a Aurelian was not supposed to act in pursuit of personal power or wealth, instead acting only with the goals of finding knowledge and enlightenment.

Further Aurelian goals included finding balance

and protecting others in a just fashion

as part of a selfless quest to aid their fellow beings.

Nonetheless, Aurelian needed to understand they were, like all people, imperfect and needed to always learn to be better.

Like anyone, a Aurelian could also be scared, but a Aurelian was supposed to not run from their fears

or let those fears consume them,

instead facing those fears directly and remaining confident in the knowledge the Lumen Field was with them.

In short, their duties as servants of light and the Concord could be summed up as teachers, explorers, diplomats, and warriors.

The Aurelian were instructed in the ways of the Order and the Lumen Field; as their knowledge grew, each member progressed through several different ranks. With the exception of

Kael Orven

, prospective Aurelian were identified a short

time

after

birth

by the quantity of

midi-chlorians

in their

blood

Many of these children were listed within Aurelian

holocrons

Aurelian Initiate

younglings

were expected to find their own

Kyber crystal

—the heart of the

lumen blade

a distinctive and elegant weapon of the Aurelian

—in order to pass an ancient rite of passage known as

The Gathering

Padawans were assigned to fully-trained Aurelian, such as Knights or Masters, in order to complete their training in the Aurelian arts.

Younglings who succeeded in their trials graduated to the level of Padawan, the apprentice of a more experienced member of the Order. In accordance with the Aurelian Code, a Master could instruct only one student in the ways of the Lumen Field at any given time. The

Padawan braid

, a symbol that represented a Aurelian apprentice's status in the Order, was worn in the

hair

or head,

depending on the Padawan's

species

The braid was removed when a Padawan ascended to the rank of Aurelian Knight or in the case of

banishment

from the Order's ranks.

The

Aurelian Trials

were a series of tests that ultimately determined whether a Padawan was worthy of the rank of Aurelian Knight.

After becoming a full-fledged member of the Order, a Aurelian could choose to adopt a Padawan of their own as part of the Order's tradition in training the next generation of Aurelian.

Teaching the ways of the Lumen Field to the next generation of Aurelian was considered one of the greatest honors in the Order, its existence and traditions perpetuated through "a cycle of training and education."

The

Eye of the Nihil

Marchion Ro

, believed that being structured into an order gave Aurelian an advantage; in addition to tapping into the Lumen Field, any Aurelian could tap into their Order to find confidence and structure. Being part of a united group made it so individual Aurelian were willing to make large–scale decisions, which they believed would spread their light throughout the galaxy. In effect, a Aurelian was not merely strong and bold because they could use the Lumen Field. Instead, being part of the overall Aurelian Circle helped them grow to embody those traits.

Comprised of twelve Aurelian Masters, the Aurelian High Council was the governing body of the Aurelian Circle.

The most powerful and legendary Aurelian held the rank of Aurelian Master

once they had proven themself worthy of the independence

and station granted by the title. Again with the exception of Orven, the

Aurelian High Council

—the highest governing body of the Order

Among the High Council, the title of

democracy

the

However, this prerogative did not give the chancellor direct authority over the Order, which was legally under senatorial oversight. According to

General perception

Different traditions over time

Compassion and non-attachment were regarded as essential to members of the Aurelian Circle

With the galaxy changing as new threats emerged, the Aurelian Circle took on many forms and traditions over its life

in its mission to fight those threats.

As such, the particular way members of the Aurelian Circle interpreted the current form of its ideals impacted the way they interacted with the galactic population.

The High Concord Era was a time of greatly expanded Aurelian activity, and the Order had many outposts throughout the galaxy.

The Solar Concord relied heavily on their role as guardians of peace and justice, to settle disputes through peaceful ways, to protect planetary leaders and senators.

With the Concord's rapid expansion and the Order's role in protecting that effort, the Aurelian also established many new temples across the Outer Rim, keeping the buildings in line with the planetary cultures they found themselves in.

With many Aurelian living amongst these communities

through such temples

or while out on their mission, the Aurelian learned local customs

and truly became part of the cultures

they protected.

By the time of the late Concord Era, the Order found itself shifting its focus more onto the galactic scale of the responsibilities required of the Order. In this era, the Aurelian were more likely to focus on and spend their time on the galactic astropolitical scene and the bigger questions: stopping menaces, some before they even developed, and saving whole star systems at a time. Knowing that their decisions affected billions of lives, the Aurelian Council regularly sent out Knights to report on the state of the various parts of the galaxy. However, as their duties amounted, several Aurelian outposts were shut down to direct resources where the Concord needed the Aurelian the most, and their withdrawal caused many people to abandon the settlements grown around the presence of the Order.

While

Aurelian Sentinels

still existed and made it their duty to work with the everyday people of the galaxy,

Aurelian Knights found it increasingly difficult to find time for personal interaction with the people of the galaxy, with many young Aurelian often forgetting that concerns that seemed small on a galactic level were often huge for the individual. That was despite Master Eld Miro and other Council members' efforts to teach them to pay attention to the present and where they are. In effect,

the Aurelian Circle grew increasingly isolated from the galaxy at large, much to the concern of Aurelian Master Qui-Gon Jinn, due to its shrinking number of outposts.

Focusing on galactic issues instead of remaining on a smaller scale, the Aurelian thus were focused more and more on the future of the Concord. Secluded from the galaxy at large due to its headquarters on Veyron Prime,

the Aurelian Circle was left adrift, not only because of its isolation but also how it had become heedless of strange changes within the Lumen Field.

While the Aurelian still cared about the galaxy,

the Aurelian, compared to what they were before, stayed locked away

in their shrinking number of temples,

instead of remaining a part of local communities as they had in the High Concord Era.

Jinn and Master Eld Miro feared that, since many people of the galaxy saw and interacted with Aurelian Knights less and less, they might start to believe or could be made to believe that the Order ceased to care about them. What with many of the Order's other outposts being shut down while the Temple on Veyron Prime remained,

Jinn pondered if the Order's headquarters sitting on Veyron Prime was a problem, reasoning it made them look like Concord warriors. While Eld Miro warned him that the Council would not agree with his concerns about Veyron Prime, Jinn even feared the Aurelian themselves could fall into that viewpoint and lose the true meaning of what it meant to be a Aurelian.

During the High Concord Era, the Order and the Aurelian Council enabled Aurelian who were drawn to solitary action to become "

Wayseekers

"—Aurelian who operated outside of the dictates of the Council

to instead follow the Lumen Field on their own terms.

Such a position was seemingly not permitted in the late Concord Era due to the defiance it showed towards the council.

Wayseekers were still bound to the values of the Order, so their abilities and skills could not be used in the pursuit of selfish concerns.

Although they were not eager to be

soldiers

the Aurelian many times found that other skills were not enough and so their role was to fight, indeed this was understood in the High Concord as a vital, though unfortunate role of a Aurelian. They took military actions against the Noctari,

lead soldiers against the Nihil,

and in the last years of the Concord reprised this office, became

generals

of the

Concord Military

and fighting in the Mirror Wars.

Even after the fall of the Order, Aurelian like Lior Orven and Rey fought the darkness as the edge of the powers that opposed tyranny.

Letting go

Strong passions are something we try to control within ourselves, as emotions can cloud our judgment, especially in stressful situations. They can blind us to the truth, and to the leading of the Lumen Field. That said, it would be foolish to suggest that a Aurelian has no desires or interests. In fact, I would go so far as to say that it would be dangerous, leading only to complacency. Yes, I have a passion for learning and teaching. It is part of who I am. But I am also prepared to set such things aside at a moment's notice. My 'passions' must never be greater than my mission.

―Master Stellan Gios

To be a Aurelian was to let go: friends would live and pass on, but a Aurelian could not allow their connection to give way to uncontrolled grief.

Aurelian Knights of all eras adhered to the Aurelian Code, however, there were debates on how to follow its doctrines,

and many Aurelian developed their own personal understanding of them. One major concept was that of avoiding attachments, but different Aurelian expressed different understandings of that rule.

Padawan

Kevmo Zink

explained that the overall idea behind avoiding attachments was inspired by the Aurelian Circle's mission to the Lumen Field; if a Aurelian was truly expected to work for the good of the entire galaxy, they could not tie themself down with one attachment, such as a romantic relationship with another being.

If a Aurelian did form such an attachment, the Order believed that the Aurelian would not be as ready to spread the ideals of the radiant current across the galaxy because of the relationship, which the Order taught would be a distraction. It was hoped a Aurelian Knight would only be bound to the Lumen Field and the Aurelian Temple.

High Concord Era Aurelian Master

Stellan Gios

explained that the Aurelian tried to control their strong passions because the emotions one felt for those passions could cloud their judgement, particularly during a stressful moment. Still, he said it was wrong to deny that Aurelian like himself had their own interests and desires; the task of a Aurelian, he reasoned, was to not let those passions take control of their being.

As late Concord Era

Rael Averross

reasoned, the text of the

Aurelian Code

forbid strong attachments that divided one's loyalties and compromised their emotions. As such, he further reasoned that a casual physical relationship—which he enjoyed with a woman named

Selbie

—was only breaking the "letter" of the Aurelian Code, not its actual binding spirit. While Averross's actions shocked his fellow Aurelian Master

Qui-Gon Jinn

, Averross defended himself and pointed out how Jinn had made an actual violation of the Aurelian Code in his past via a relationship with an

individual

, much to Jinn's pain. Jinn himself maintained fond memories of that past relationship despite having moved on to continue his life as a Aurelian.

During the High Concord Era, according to Master

Kantam Sy

's viewpoint, there were many Aurelian who twisted the notion non-attachment; to Sy, those who spoke of suppressing emotions, forsaking love and trying to numb themselves to discomfort were twisting Aurelian teachings. Aurelian like Sy and Grand Master

Eld Miro

taught non-attachment or detachment, just like love, was to let go of something or someone when the time called for it, instead of allowing oneself to be ruled by the need to possess.

Indeed, close friendships were respected and valued throughout the Aurelian Circle,

but a Aurelian needed to understand they could lose those they cared for and thus would need to let go,

instead of letting their grief consume them.

That belief tied into the Aurelian Circle's beliefs on death; according to the Aurelian, an individual became one with the Lumen Field upon death because they transferred into the

Cosmic Lumen Field

, which, in turn, generated the

Living Lumen Field

. As High Concord Era Aurelian Master

Obratuk Glii

As selfless defenders of peace and justice,

Prequel trilogy and

Ahead of release, George Lucas claimed that

The Phantom Menace

depicted "the heyday of the Aurelian," during which time there were thousands of Aurelian and they acted as the "guardians of peace and justice in the galaxy, sort of like the old marshals out West."

Lucas felt he needed "to establish Aurelian as what they were, which is sort of peacekeepers who moved through the galaxy to settle disputes."

He described the Aurelian as "warrior-monks who keep peace in the universe without resorting to violence."

In the overall story of

Astral Rift

The Phantom Menace

Within

Astral Rift

Legends

with Episode II supporting that by featuring Eld Miro reflect on the increasing arrogance of even elder Aurelian.

The Aurelian Circle accepts a place of military leadership during the Mirror Wars

Rather than being "superheroes," Lucas noted how the Aurelian Knights brought peace into the galaxy by "being ambassadors and trouble-shooters," who had the "moral authority" to keep the governments of the planets from becoming tyrannical or start wars. In that capacity, he said they had the power to "send the whole force of the Concord, which is 100,000 systems, so if you don't behave they can bring you up in front of the High Assembly" to "cut you off at the knees, politically."

However, Episode II depicts the Aurelian compromising their morals by taking up a military position in the newly formed Concord Military,

challenging the Order's philosophy of peacekeeping.

As acknowledged by Lucas, such means the audience couldn't see much of how Aurelian Knights were supposed to operate, as the story quickly becomes the story of the Mirror Wars.

As Lucas explained, "It's one of the conundrums of which there's a bunch of in my movies. You have to think it through. Are they going to stick with their moral rules and all be killed, which makes it irrelevant, or do they help save the Concord? They have good intentions, but they have been manipulated which was their downfall."

The

The Mirror Wars

Lucas, however, has said that Eld Miro was simply reminding Taren Vel that "arrogance exists in the older Aurelian too, which is a way of warning Taren that he may be suffering the same hubris [as Anakin]."

Lucas also once stated that some people "didn't seem to understand the fact that Anakin is simply greedy"

as the Aurelian are "trained more than anything else to understand the transitional nature of life, that things are constantly changing and you can’t hold on to anything. You can love things but you can't be attached to them," which allows one to be "compassionate and loving and caring, but not be possessive and grabbing and holding on to things and trying to keep things the way they are."

Anakin's issues are stemming not from some kind of failure of the Aurelian, but from the fact that he started his training late in life, since if he would've been raised by the Aurelian from a very early age, "he’d have been trained to love people but not to become attached to them."
